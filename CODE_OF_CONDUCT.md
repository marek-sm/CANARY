# Code of Conduct

CANARY is a collaborative research and engineering project. Participation requires professional, respectful conduct and strict adherence to the project's safety and research-integrity boundaries.

## Expected behavior

- Critique ideas, code, methods, and evidence without attacking people.
- Give accurate credit and obtain consent before publishing a person's name or contribution statement.
- Protect personal information, private communications, credentials, restricted sources, and unreleased evidence.
- Raise safety, validity, attribution, accessibility, and workload concerns early.
- Correct mistakes transparently and preserve the history of material decisions.
- Accept work only after reviewing the full commitment, then own its completion criteria and communicate immediately if unavoidable circumstances threaten delivery.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, discrimination, intimidation, personal attacks, or retaliation.
- Fabricating, suppressing, cherry-picking, or knowingly misrepresenting evidence.
- Testing third-party systems without explicit written permission.
- Publishing credentials, personal data, private personnel material, restricted payloads, or raw unreleased results.
- Claiming sole credit for team results or attributing work without consent.
- Pressuring a contributor to approve a claim, source, or release they believe is unsupported.

## Scope

This policy applies in the repository, issues, pull requests, reviews, project meetings, demonstrations, publications, and project-related public communication.

## Reporting

Report conduct concerns privately to the project lead or appropriate CS + AI Club leadership through an existing private organizational channel. A reporter without access to such a channel may open a public issue requesting a private contact route, but must not include complaint details there. A person involved in a complaint must not be the sole decision-maker for that complaint; use an uninvolved club leader or institutional process when necessary.

## Response

Responses should be proportionate and may include clarification, correction, content removal, a warning, restricted participation, removal from the project space, or escalation to the relevant club or university process. Security incidents also follow `SECURITY.md`; research-integrity or release defects follow `SPEC.md` Section 11.
